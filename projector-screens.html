Buying Guide

Projector Screens

The screen is half the image. A good projector on a bad surface looks bad; a decent projector on a good screen looks far better than its price suggests. Here's how to choose.

Screen materials, translated

  • Matte white (gain 1.0): the neutral default. Accurate colors, wide viewing angles. Best for dark rooms.
  • Gray: deepens blacks and adds perceived contrast — helpful if your projector's black levels are mediocre or the room isn't pitch dark.
  • ALR (ambient light rejecting): optically rejects off-axis room light. Genuinely effective in living rooms, but expensive, with narrower viewing angles and a premium price.
  • Gain above 1.0: brighter image, but hot-spotting and narrower viewing angles. Rarely worth it for home use.

Formats

  • Fixed-frame: a perfectly flat, tensioned surface — the best image quality. Needs a dedicated wall.
  • Pull-down / motorized: disappears when not in use. Great for living rooms; make sure it's tab-tensioned or expect waves.
  • Portable / tripod: for backyards and travel. Fine for fun, not for critical viewing.
  • Paint: special screen paint on a smooth wall lands between a bare wall and a real screen. Only worth it with proper prep.

When a plain wall is fine

A smooth, matte, neutral-white or light-gray wall is a perfectly respectable screen for casual viewing — especially while you decide on size. What ruins it: texture, color tint, and sheen. If your wall is orange-peel textured or eggshell, a real screen is a bigger upgrade than a brighter projector.

Sizing

Pick screen size from your seating distance and your projector's throw range — not from ambition. Our Distance Guide calculator does the math both ways.
