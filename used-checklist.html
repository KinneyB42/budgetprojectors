The Used Projector Buying Checklist

The used market is where the real deals live. A three-year-old mid-range projector often beats a brand-new budget model at the same price. But used buying has rules. Follow this checklist and you will not get burned.

The checklist

  1. Stick to Buy It Now listings. Auctions look exciting and end in overbidding. Buy It Now listings let you research calmly, message the seller, and use Best Offer. Patience beats bidding wars.
  2. Find the lamp hours. This is the odometer. It's in the projector's menu under info or status. Ask the seller for a photo of it. Under 2,000 hours on a lamp rated for 4,000+ is healthy. Over 4,000 on the original lamp means you're buying a replacement lamp too — factor that into the price.
  3. Price a replacement lamp before you buy. Look up what a new lamp costs for that exact model. If the projector is $200 and the lamp is $150, your real price is $350. Sometimes that still wins. Sometimes it doesn't.
  4. Confirm the remote is included. A missing remote is a real cost — some replacements run $30–50. If the listing doesn't show one in the photos or mention it in the description, assume there isn't one and price a compatible replacement, or ask the seller directly.
  5. Read the condition description like a lawyer. "Tested and working" is the minimum. Look for mentions of dust blobs (bright spots on the image), color wheel noise, fan noise, or missing lens caps. Vague one-line descriptions with stock photos are a pass.
  6. Check the seller, not just the price. Established feedback with projector or electronics sales is ideal. A seller with no feedback selling a used (not parts-only) projector is fine — but confirm the condition with them in writing first if there are no returns.
  7. Use Best Offer instead of paying asking price. Most used listings have room. Offer 10–15% under asking as a starting point and let the seller counter. The worst they can do is say no.
  8. Factor in shipping and import fees. Projectors are heavy and shipping adds up. Listings shipping from overseas are fine as long as the import fee is included in the shipping cost — check before you commit.
  9. Test it the day it arrives. Power it on, check every input, look for dust blobs on a white screen, listen for grinding or whining. Your return window starts ticking at delivery, so don't let it sit in the box.

Red flags: walk away

  • "For parts / not working / untested." Unless you repair projectors for fun, this is someone else's problem.
  • No returns plus a vague description. One of those alone is manageable. Both together is a gamble.
  • Stock photos only. If the seller won't photograph the actual unit, they don't want you seeing it.
  • Lamp hours "unknown" and they won't check. Checking takes 30 seconds in the menu. Refusing to check tells you everything.
  • Price barely below new. If a used unit is only 10% cheaper than new, buy new and get the warranty.

What to message the seller

Copy, paste, and fill in the blanks:

Hi — interested in the projector. Could you tell me: 1) the lamp hours from the menu, 2) whether the original remote is included, 3) if there are any dust blobs, image issues, or unusual fan noise? Thanks!

A good seller answers all three. A seller who dodges them just saved you money.
