How to read this

Lens shift moves the image with glass

Lens shift physically repositions the lens, so the whole image moves without changing shape. If your placement fits inside the model’s published shift range, the picture stays square at full resolution. It is the only correction that costs you nothing, which is why this calculator checks it first.

Keystone moves it with software

Keystone correction reshapes the image digitally to undo a trapezoid. It works, but the corrected picture is smaller than the one the projector threw, and the compressed side gives up sharpness and resolution. A few degrees is a fair trade for a couch-side setup. A lot of degrees is a sign the projector is in the wrong spot, or is the wrong model for the room.

Pointing straight vs. turned

A projector throws its image where the lens points. Leave it pointing straight ahead from an off-center spot and the image lands off center with it; lens shift is what can pull it back. Turn the projector to face the screen and the image centers, but the beam arrives at an angle and the rectangle becomes a trapezoid. Most real rooms end up using one approach or the other, and the verdict above tells you which one your placement needs.
