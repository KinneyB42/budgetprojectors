Buying Guide

Audio Solutions

Here's the open secret of projectors: the built-in speakers are almost always terrible. A 100-inch image with phone-quality sound isn't a home theater — it's a disappointment. Budget for audio from day one; it's the highest-impact upgrade you can make.

The three sensible paths

  • Soundbar: the simplest real upgrade. One box, one cable (HDMI ARC if your projector supports it), dramatically better than built-ins. Ideal for living rooms and bedrooms.
  • Powered bookshelf speakers: a pair of self-amplified speakers gives you true stereo separation and fuller sound than any soundbar at the same price. Great for desks and small rooms.
  • AV receiver + passive speakers: the full home theater route — 3.1 or 5.1 surround, real bass, room to grow. More money and more wires, but nothing else sounds like it.

What to watch for with projectors specifically

  • Audio delay: Bluetooth audio from a projector often lags behind the picture. A wired connection (HDMI ARC, optical, or aux) avoids lip-sync issues.
  • Volume control: make sure you can control the external speaker's volume conveniently — ideally through the projector remote via HDMI-CEC/ARC.
  • Placement: sound should come from near the screen, not from behind you at the projector. Center-channel clarity matters more than rumble for dialogue.
  • Outdoors: open air eats bass. A portable PA-style speaker or a punchy Bluetooth speaker (wired in, to avoid delay) beats a soundbar outside.

How much should you spend?

A good rule of thumb: allocate roughly a third of your total budget to audio. A $600 projector with a $200 soundbar will make you happier than an $800 projector with no audio plan.

Matching audio to your exact room and projector? That's what consultations are for.
