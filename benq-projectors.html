Buying Guide

BenQ Projectors

BenQ is one of the most recommended brands in the projector world — and for good reason. They make DLP projectors across home entertainment, gaming, and business categories, and they're known for accurate out-of-the-box color, which is rarer than it should be at budget prices.

Know which BenQ line you're looking at

  • Home entertainment: tuned for movies and TV, usually the best color accuracy of the three lines. The right choice for a living room or dedicated theater.
  • Gaming: prioritizes low input lag and high refresh rates. Worth it if you play fast-paced games; overkill if you don't.
  • Business / education: built for bright rooms and presentations. High brightness, but color accuracy takes a back seat — not ideal for movie nights.

The specs that actually matter

  • Resolution: true 1080p is the sweet spot for budget buyers. "Supported 4K" on a 1080p chip is marketing — it accepts a 4K signal but displays 1080p.
  • Brightness (lumens): match it to your room. A dark theater needs far less than a living room with windows. More lumens isn't automatically better — it washes out blacks in dark rooms.
  • Throw ratio: determines how far back the projector sits for your screen size. Measure your room first — use our Distance Guide.
  • Input lag: matters for gaming (look for under ~30ms); irrelevant for movies.
  • Lamp vs. LED/laser: traditional lamps are bright and cheap to replace; LED and laser last far longer with no lamp swaps.

What to watch out for

  • Don't pay extra for smart features you'll never use — a streaming stick is cheaper and better.
  • Built-in speakers are a backup, not a solution. Budget for real audio (see our Audio Solutions guide).
  • Check the warranty and lamp replacement cost before buying — a cheap projector with an expensive lamp isn't cheap.
